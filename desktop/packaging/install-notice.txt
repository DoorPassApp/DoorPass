Before you install DoorPass

WHAT DOORPASS CHANGES ON THIS COMPUTER

Installing DoorPass copies the app to your user folder and adds a Start menu shortcut. Nothing else changes until you open DoorPass.

The first time you open it, DoorPass asks for administrator permission to install its helper: the "DoorPass Helper" Windows service in C:\Program Files\DoorPass Helper, together with the Wintun virtual network adapter from WireGuard.

While protection is turned on, the helper:
  - points your computer's DNS settings at DoorPass's local tunnel, and
  - in Full mode, routes your IPv4 traffic through that tunnel on this computer so it can split the TLS handshake of HTTPS connections.
Turning protection off, or quitting DoorPass, puts these settings back.

WHAT DOORPASS SENDS, AND WHERE

DoorPass sends nothing until you turn protection on. While it is on, your DNS lookups are sent, encrypted, to the DNS-over-HTTPS server selected in DoorPass. The default is Google Public DNS; you can choose another server, or your own, before turning protection on. That server's own privacy policy applies to those lookups.

DoorPass has no analytics, telemetry or crash reporting, and sends nothing to its developers. The optional query log (off by default) stays on this computer.

UNINSTALLING

In DoorPass, open the main window and choose Helper > Uninstall helper, then remove DoorPass from Settings > Apps > Installed apps. Uninstalling the app also offers to remove the helper.
